Vocal Synth Symposium 2026

Hosted by King’s College London Department of Digital Humanities and Vocaloid Society, the symposium features several talks and short interventions, a keynote, an industry-featured talk, and a roundtable Q&A.

Rongzhi Zhou

A former social science student who studied at the University of Warwick and University of Nottingham (in Ningbo, China). Now working at a Chinese company to sell refrigerators abroad. Abstract

This research is a fandom study that aims to unpack how UK members experience belonging to the fan community of Vocaloid, a niche yet expanding genre in the West within the Japanese media landscape. Through the interplay of belonging, collective identity, and a sense of community, the study explores how these transcultural fans negotiate fan identity and attach to a niche community. Using thematic analysis of 10 in-depth semi-structured interviews with members from Vocaloid societies in UK universities, the qualitative research employs an interpretivist paradigm to gain an in-depth understanding of subjective experiences and perspectives of individuals in the UK Vocaloid fan community.

Illinois Vocaloid Research Group

University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC)

The Illinois Vocaloid Research Group is a Registered Student Organization at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. The purpose of the group is to promote Cultural Studies and Multi-media Creations around the culture of Synthetic Vocals.


Abstract

In Vocaloid culture, “covers” play a central role in helping songs travel beyond their original versions. Through fan recreations and translations, Vocaloid music reaches new audiences and supports the growth of an international fandom. However, Japanese-to-English fan covers often reveal conflicts in cross-cultural understanding, especially when lyrical imagery depends on specific historical or cultural backgrounds. In this presentation, we focus on “Summertime Record” and “Senbonzakura” to examine how such misunderstandings appear, why translation in interlingual covers is especially difficult, and how better translations might be created through deeper cultural awareness. This is not a formal linguistic study, but a reflection on issues noticed by our club members, intended to raise awareness of cultural mismatch in fan translation.

Zirui Chen

Loughborough University

Zirui Chen began her doctoral research in Communication and Media at Loughborough University in October 2023. Her research examines virtual idol fan practices on Chinese social media, with a particular focus on fan prosumption and fandom nationalism. Zirui’s research interests include digital culture, fan practices and virtual idols.


Abstract

The spectrum of fan prosumption practices in Luo Tianyi fandom reveals a fluid continuum rather than fixed categories. It provides the empirical foundation for understanding what fans do and why they prefer virtual idols for their features of personalisation and authenticity. Besides, this research also considers “how” these practices are socially organised and negotiated. It argues that Luo Tianyi’s fandom constitutes a stratified social space where fandom hierarchies, gender norms and ongoing negotiations with the media company coexist and shape fans’ participatory experiences.
